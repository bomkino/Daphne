# Daphne brand

How Daphne looks, sounds and moves, with the reason for each rule. Everything in this folder is built from code in `tools/`, so a change to the mark is a change to `tools/mark.mjs`, never a hand edit to an SVG. Open `index.html` for the brand book: the same rules, shown. Everything here is CC0: free to take, change and use.

## The idea

**A laurel is two leaves around a name.** Strip away the gold and the ceremony, and that's what a festival laurel does: it holds a film's name between two branches and says someone chose it. So Daphne's logo is the name held between two bay leaves, drawn as brackets: **(Daphne)**.

The name comes from Greek myth. Daphne turned into the first laurel tree, and Apollo, the god of the arts, wore her leaves and crowned artists with them from then on. Every laurel since comes from her. Daphne the tool gives that laurel to every filmmaker who has earned one, for free.

The brackets are also a system. Anything worth honoring can sit between the two leaves: (Official Selection), (your film), (free). The logo shows the product's whole job in one shape.

## The story

The brand book (`index.html`) opens with Daphne's myth, told in one continuous line of light that you scroll like a film. Each line is a subtitle; the picture underneath keeps changing shape without a cut:

| Line | What you see |
|---|---|
| Daphne was a river nymph who wanted to belong to no one. | Nine hairlines of light flow across the dark like a river: her hair and the water at once. |
| When a god chased her, she begged to be changed. Her arms became branches. Her hair became leaves. | The river quickens, then rises into a tree with raised arms. Leaves grow along the branches one after another, each from its stalk. |
| She became the first laurel tree. Her heart, the story goes, still beat under the bark. | Everything stills. Under the trunk, a Bloom glow beats, lub-dub, at 52 beats a minute. It's the only pink in the film. |
| The god wore her leaves as a crown. Since then, laurels have gone to the poets, athletes and artists who were chosen. | The branches bend into a laurel and lift as a crown. Faint copies ripple out from it: every laurel given since. |
| Every laurel since is hers. She still belongs to no one, so now she's free to every filmmaker who's earned one. | The leaves fall, all but two. Those two travel to the logo's brackets, and her name rises between them. |

It ends on the logo, the tagline and `pitch.dog/daphne`. The heartbeat is from Ovid's *Metamorphoses*, Book I: Apollo lays his hand on the trunk and feels her heart still trembling under the new bark.

Tell the story where there's room for it: the brand book, a launch film, the about page. Everywhere else, the plain words come first.

## Name and words

| | Use |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Daphne. Always capitalized, never "DAPHNE" in running text, never "the Daphne". |
| **Tagline** | Make festival laurels for your film. It's free. |
| **Descriptor** | Daphne · Free festival laurel maker for filmmakers. For page titles, search results, app stores and bios. |
| **Headline** | Your film did the hard part. Now make the laurel. Optional, for the app's welcome and launch posts. |
| **About** | Daphne makes festival laurels, the wreaths on a film's poster that say it was selected for, or won at, a festival. Type the festival, your selection and the year, pick a look, and download it for your poster, trailer and socials as a PNG, SVG, PDF or video. It's free and works in your browser, with no account and no watermark. Nothing you make leaves your device unless you share it. Made by pitch.dog, and open source. |
| **Why the name** | Why Daphne? In Greek myth, Daphne turned into the first laurel tree. Every laurel since comes from her. |
| **Credit line** | Made with Daphne. Optional, text only, for people who want to credit the tool. Never a logo on someone's laurel. |

Rules, and why:

- **The name never travels alone.** Daphne is a person's name, not a description, so a title, H1, social card, bio or video end card always carries the tagline or the descriptor beside it. Someone seeing it for the first time should know what it is without clicking.
- **Plain words first.** Say what it makes, for whom, and what it costs. The freedoms are the selling point, so name them: no account, no watermark, nothing uploaded.
- **One voice.** We're pitch.dog, in the plural ("we made Daphne"). Name no one on the team. US spelling, and dates like October 31, 2026.
- **Honest.** Daphne makes the laurels people have earned. Brand material never shows a real festival's name or artwork, and never suggests a selection someone doesn't have. Sample laurels say "Your Festival".

## The logo

`logo/daphne-logo-on-dark.svg` · `logo/daphne-logo-on-light.svg` · `logo/daphne-logo-mono.svg` (takes the text color) · black and white versions · the hero versions, `logo/daphne-logo-hero-on-dark.svg` and `-on-light.svg`, whose leaves carry their side veins, for 400 px wide and up · PNGs in `logo/png/`.

- **Construction.** The name is PD Head at weight 400, outlined, tracked in by 6 units: light enough to feel classical, heavy enough to hold at 96 px. Each leaf is a bay leaf on a circular spine: a rounded base, widest a little below the middle, a long acute tip, a hairline midrib cut out of it, and a short stalk that bends in under the name. The leaves are slightly lopsided, like real bay leaves. The gap to the D is wider than the gap to the e, because the D's flat side needs more air than the e's round one.
- **Two colors.** On Grove, the name is Paper and the leaves are New leaf. On Paper or white, the name is Grove and the leaves are Bay. The name and the leaves can share one color (the mono, black and white files) wherever two colors aren't possible.
- **Clear space.** Keep one leaf's width clear on every side: about a fifth of the logo's height.
- **Smallest size.** 96 px wide on screen, 20 mm in print. Below that the leaves read as plain parentheses: use the symbol.
- **Don't.** Recolor the leaves gold or Bloom. Swap the leaves for typed parentheses. Stretch, rotate, outline or shadow it. Set the name in another face. Put the logo on a laurel someone made with Daphne.

## The symbol

`symbol/daphne-symbol-*.svg`, `symbol/daphne-avatar.svg` (profile pictures), `symbol/daphne-app-icon.svg`, and the favicon set in `favicon/`.

**(D)**: the D in PD Head 600 between fuller leaves whose stalks nearly meet under the letter, like a wreath's crossed stems. It's for anywhere square: avatars, app icons, the browser tab.

- Profile pictures are cropped to a circle, so the avatar keeps the symbol inside the middle 64%.
- From 40 px down, use the small symbol (`symbol/daphne-symbol-small.svg`, used by `favicon/favicon.svg`): a heavier D and leaves with no midrib or stalk, because those fill in at 16 px.

## Color

| Name | Hex | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Grove** | `#12291E` | The ground. A laurel grove at night: dark enough for cinema, green enough to be ours. |
| **Grove deep** | `#0B1B13` | Grove's shadow, for vignettes and the edges of a frame. |
| **Paper** | `#F3F6EF` | Words on Grove, and the light ground. A cool, leafy white, never cream. |
| **New leaf** | `#B8E59A` | The leaves on Grove. Young bay leaves are this color. |
| **Bay** | `#1F5C3B` | The leaves on Paper and white. |
| **Bloom** | `#F59CC0` | Her heart. The Daphne flower blooms pink. One accent, used once per frame. |

Contrast (WCAG): Paper on Grove 14.1 · New leaf on Grove 10.8 · Bloom on Grove 7.6 · Bay on Paper 7.3 · Bay on white 7.9. Bloom on Paper is 1.9, so **Bloom is never text on a light ground**; on light, Bloom can only be a fill behind Grove text.

Rules, and why:

- **Gold belongs to the laurels people make.** Every festival laurel is gold or silver, so the brand never is. Green is the living tree; gold is the honor someone earned. When Daphne's material shows a laurel, the laurel is the only metal in the frame.
- **Grove leads.** Most frames are Grove. Paper is for print, documents and the occasional light card in a set, so the dark ones stay special.
- **One Bloom.** A highlighted word, a "Free" tag, a single flower. If a frame has two Bloom things, one goes.

Tokens: `tokens/daphne.tokens.json` (design tokens) and `tokens/daphne.css` (CSS custom properties plus the font faces).

## Type

Daphne is made by pitch.dog and speaks in pitch.dog's type: the [pitch.dog type system](https://github.com/bomkino/pitchdog-type-system) v3.0.0. Its font files are CC0 and copied into `fonts/` so the app and these files can use them; the system's code, tokens and docs are not, so they stay in their own repository.

| Role | Face | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Display | PD Head 400, 600 | The wordmark (400), the symbol's D (600), headlines, slide titles. Big and few. Story lines can go down to 300. |
| Lead | PD Body Alt 400 | The tagline and large explanatory lines. |
| Text | PD Body 400, 600 | Interface, captions, running text. |
| Data | PD Eyebrow 500 | Formats, sizes, dates, file names, the URL: `PNG · 4000 px`, `pitch.dog/daphne`. |

- Sentence case everywhere. Headlines end with a period when they're sentences.
- The laurels themselves use whatever face the filmmaker chooses. Brand type never goes inside a user's laurel.

## The leaf brackets

`bracket/leaf-left.svg` and `bracket/leaf-right.svg` are the logo's leaves on their own, drawn on PD Head's em: set them at `height: 1.24em; vertical-align: -0.30em` beside PD Head type and they sit like parentheses.

- **Hold one thing.** One bracketed phrase per frame, slide or page, around the thing being honored or given: (your film), (Official Selection), (free).
- New leaf on Grove, Bay on Paper. Never Bloom, never gold.
- In running text, ordinary parentheses stay ordinary. The leaves are for display type only.

## Laurels in the brand

Daphne's best advertising is the laurels it makes, so show them.

- Every laurel in brand material is made with Daphne, with its own presets (`tools/laurels.html` renders the samples in `laurels/`). Say which look it is when that helps: Classic Gold, Black Tie.
- Sample words are placeholders nobody could mistake for a real selection: "Official Selection · Your Festival · 2026".
- No real festival names, logos or artwork. A real film's poster appears only with its makers' agreement.
- A laurel sits on Grove with nothing else metal near it, and big enough to read its smallest line.

## Motion

`motion/reveal.html` (open it and scrub), `motion/daphne-reveal.mp4`, `motion/daphne-reveal.gif`. Growth paths for both leaves: `motion/logo-paths.json`.

1. **Things grow from where they're attached.** A leaf grows from its stalk to its tip, along its own spine, with a soft growing edge. Nothing pops in, scales from its center or slides in from off screen.
2. **Leaves first, then words.** The leaves make room; the name rises into it. In the reveal, the leaves grow for 1.05 s and the name starts at 0.5 s.
3. **One leaf per frame.** When a full wreath grows, it adds one leaf per frame at 24 fps: a wreath of 24 leaves grows in exactly one second of film.
4. **Letters arrive one at a time,** two frames apart, rising 70 units (a tenth of the cap height) as they fade in.
5. **Easing.** Growth: `cubic-bezier(.2, .7, .1, 1)`, quick to start and long to settle. Words: `cubic-bezier(.33, 0, .15, 1)`. No bounces, no overshoot.
6. **Her heart still beats.** In a long hold, the logo breathes with her heartbeat (lub-dub, 52 a minute), by less than half a percent. If anything glows Bloom, it's that beat, once.
7. **Let one leaf fall.** When a piece ends, a single leaf can drift down through the frame. It's the only exit we use.
8. **Hold the last frame** for at least a second, so the name can be read.
9. **Reduced motion** shows the finished frame, and the story moves only when you scroll.

A starting point for a 5-second intro or outro (the piece decides): the leaves grow (0–1.1 s), Daphne rises (0.5–1.2 s), the tagline arrives under it (1.4–2.0 s), hold, then `pitch.dog/daphne` and "Made by pitch.dog" (from 3.6 s), and hold to the end, the leaves breathing once.

**The sting** (`motion/sting/`, films in `motion/`: `daphne-intro-*.mp4` and `daphne-outro-*.mp4` at 1080×1920, 1080×1080 and 1920×1080): Daphne's 5-second intro and outro. A gold laurel made with Daphne comes alive on her heartbeat, its leaves fall, and two stay to hold her name: strip away the gold and the ceremony, and a laurel is two leaves around a name. The intro fades to dark for a film to cut in; the outro holds on the tagline, `pitch.dog/daphne` and "Made by pitch.dog". Source, score and how to rebuild: `motion/sting/README.md`.

Rebuild everything:

```
node brand/tools/build.mjs     # SVGs, tokens, the reveal page
node brand/tools/render.mjs    # PNGs, favicon.ico, social cards, sample laurels, reveal video (Playwright; ImageMagick and ffmpeg optional)
node brand/tools/book.mjs      # the brand book, index.html, with fonts and images inlined
```

`tools/glyphs/` holds the wordmark's outlines, taken from PD Head with HarfBuzz by `tools/glyphs.py` (needs `uharfbuzz`); rerun it only to change the weight or the letters. `tools/book/` holds the subsets of the fonts that the brand book inlines (made with fontTools' `pyftsubset`; PD Eyebrow instanced at 500), so the book stays one file.
