# Laurel Generator — Research 01 · Laurel anatomy and market

pitch.dog · 31 Oct 2026 · for the free, open-source, browser-based laurel generator

This builds on `System/01 — Usage study.md` (surfaces, sizes, the base data model, groupings). It does not repeat that study. It covers what the study lacks: how real festival laurels are laid out, the rules festivals attach to them, the rival tools, fonts and licences, and what filmmakers need to export.

**Method and limits.** Laurels were looked at through image search (Bing Images) and the pages hosting them, plus official guideline PDFs and pages, rival tool pages, font licences and platform help pages. Each observation names the search or host it came from (keys in square brackets, listed in Sources). Limits:

- Most big festivals hand out laurel packs privately (press portals, FilmFreeway Laurel Centers behind a login), so many laurels were seen as reproduced on film, distributor and festival pages, not from the festival's own pack. The year on the artwork dates each design.
- For Locarno, San Sebastián and Karlovy Vary only the festival identity was seen, not a film laurel. They are marked "identity only" and left out of the counts.
- Reddit could not be reached: it is blocked for the fetch tools and the browser pane. Forum evidence comes from IndieTalk (via search snippets), a filmmaker's blog, festival FAQs and advice sites instead.
- The counts come from a convenience sample biased by image search. Read them as rough shares, not statistics.

### 1.3 What the sample says

**The surprise: the top tier has dropped the branches.** The current designs of Cannes, Venice, Berlinale, Sundance (2024 on), TIFF (2020 on), IFFR, IDFA and Hot Docs are logo badges with no wreath. They lead with the brand mark and often with the edition number. Wreaths survive at mid-tier and indie festivals (SXSW, Tribeca, Sheffield, Palm Springs, Raindance, Slamdance, Annecy), at nearly every FilmFreeway festival, and in the uniform rows distributors redraw for posters. Across the 42 festival-issued designs: open two-branch 20, no branches 16, wide flanking 4, U or arch 2. Of the 26 indie designs, about 21 use an open two-branch wreath.

- An edition number sits beside or instead of the year in 4 designs (Venice ×2, Berlinale, Theta), in the identity of 3 more (Locarno, San Sebastián, KVIFF), and as an ordinal prefix in 2 indie ones (5th, 6TO).
- Years are sometimes abbreviated ('16, -17). In one design a laurel glyph replaces a digit.
- **Year in a ribbon**: not seen in any festival laurel. Ribbons belong to generic award badges in stock vector packs [STK-RIB].
- **Year under the wreath, outside the branches**: not seen. Text outside the wreath does appear, but above it (CPH:DOX logo, Woods Hole script).

**Lines.** Three or four text lines are typical. The maximum seen is **five text lines plus a logo or icon** (SXSW 2024 winner, SXSW 2016 Audience Award, Woods Hole 2019, Big Cross). The Usage study's cap of four lines is too tight for award laurels that carry status, award, category, festival and year.

**Type treatments.**
- **Brand-led (top tier):** the festival's own wordmark is the hero, and the status is a small sans line, sometimes in mixed case (IDFA "Official Selection", TIFF 2020).
- **FilmFreeway house style:** heavy condensed sans caps, black on white, the award word as hero ("BEST"), festival and year in smaller caps [FF-LC].
- **Classic:** serif caps with modest tracking (HollyShorts, Gold Coast, Big Cross).
- **Script accents:** "Official Selection" or "Best Short Film" in script over serif caps (Woods Hole, "★★★ Best Short film").
- **Wide condensed block:** BFI LFF and Raindance 2017, both with branch tips trimmed short.
- Small lines are nearly always caps with wide tracking. Mixed weights are normal: a bold hero, regular small lines.

**Unusual layouts worth supporting.**
1. **No branches**: a logo or text badge. This is the dominant top-tier form and the generator must offer it.
2. **Wide flanking** branches around a long text block (BFI, Raindance, Sundance 2011 and 2019).
3. **U or bowl wreath** with the logo breaking out above (CPH:DOX, Sheffield 2019).
4. **Curved text**: over a mascot (LSFF) or along a closed seal (NYF).
5. **Year as a hero numeral or split around the name** (Tribeca, Sheffield 2024, Venice edition numerals).
6. **Two-column** (SLO Film Fest): name block │ status + year.
7. **Vertical side text**: a rotated edition line (Theta).
8. **Custom branch material**: Slamdance's thorns and creature are a parody of the laurel. A "branch style" library could include non-leaf motifs.
9. **Icon slot** above the text or between words (Lund, Woods Hole reel).
10. **Stacks, grids and tiered rows**: one poster carried about 20 laurels in a grid ("Teacher of Patience" [IS19], [IS24]). Multiple awards inside **one** wreath were not seen in the sample. Films that win several awards show one laurel per award.

## 2. Official-laurel rules and guardrails

### 2.1 What festivals actually say

| Source | Rule | What it means for the generator |
|---|---|---|
| **Sundance** official laurel guidelines (2024 pack) [SUN-PDF] | Only for Sundance official selections and award winners, and only to promote the film. Not on merchandise or goods for sale. "Should be used without substantial alterations." "Sundance Film Festival" is a trademark and **may not be shortened to "Sundance"**. The laurel must not be altered to show another festival. Altered laurels need written approval from laurels@sundance.org. It must be **at least as large as any other festival's laurel** on the same materials. Keep the selection confidential until the public announcement. A separate winner laurel arrives by the end of the festival. | Recreating a Sundance laurel is out unless Sundance approves it in writing. "Use short names" (Usage study §8) conflicts with Sundance's rule. Row layouts must allow one laurel to be larger, or at least never smaller. |
| **Sundance** trademark page [SUN-TM] | Accepted artists may use the official laurels "in accordance with our guidelines". Any use suggesting endorsement or affiliation "will be considered trademark infringement". | The tool must not look like it issues or endorses laurels. |
| **Cannes** 2026 feature regulations, Art. 9 [CAN-REG] | Every Official Selection film must carry the Festival de Cannes "Official Selection" logo on its publicity, **especially the official poster**, plus any awards won, and a title card in the opening credits. The Festival supplies the logos and title cards through its My Account portal. | Using the official logo is **mandatory**, so a self-made Cannes laurel is wrong as well as risky. Prompt the user to place the supplied file. |
| **Cannes Short Film Corner** [SFC] | A market, not a selection. Entrants get a plain Cannes logo that is "laurel-free". | Warn when "Official Selection" is combined with "Short Film Corner", "Marché" or "Market". |
| **Berlinale** general guidelines §3.4 [BER-GL] | Participants must make sure "the official festival logo is clearly visible on all advertising and promotional measures". "The Berlinale provides specific logos for each section." | Mandatory official artwork, one per section. The generator should not offer a Berlinale preset. |
| **Giffoni** [GIF] | Submitters undertake to display the official GFF laurel on all advertising, including posters and trailers. | Same pattern: mandatory official artwork. |
| **Transilvania IFF** regulations [TIFF-RO] | Once selected, the film may use the festival's logo and the relevant laurel for publicity. | Permission depends on selection. |
| **Tallahassee FF** FAQ [TAL] | On making your own version of its laurels: "We would prefer if you didn't." | Smaller festivals also care. |
| **FilmQuest** laurel page [FQ] | Supplies PNGs and tells filmmakers to recolour them with a Photoshop Color Overlay. | Some festivals invite recolouring, so a "recolour official PNG" feature is legitimate for those. |
| **FilmFreeway** Laurel Center [FF-HELP] | Festivals upload laurels or build them with FilmFreeway's own Laurel Creation Tool, and can password-protect the Center. | The FilmFreeway laurel is the official artwork for thousands of small festivals. |
| **IndieTalk** forum thread [IT] | Practitioners say it is very common to have a designer restyle small festivals' laurels to match a poster, and nobody had heard of trouble for doing so. One commenter advised against altering prestigious festivals' laurels. | This matches the distributor-made uniform rows seen in §1.2. |
| **Dark Skies** advice [DSK] | Ask the festival for high-res artwork first. If none comes, a self-made laurel should accurately reflect the festival's branding and award, ideally sent to the festival for approval. Show few, strong laurels. | Supports an "I asked the festival / it supplied nothing" flow. |

### 2.2 Laurel inflation and laurel mills

- **Award mills.** Pay-to-enter "festivals" hand out laurels so that filmmakers will post them, which in turn advertises the festival. Warning signs include many vague categories, no public screenings, "awards only" or "online only" events, calls open all year, and names that echo famous festivals [SMS], [GSH]. Go Short quotes a journalist: winners "get an A4 sheet and a PDF file containing a laurel" [GSH].
- **Borrowed prestige.** In the sample, a "World Film Festival in Cannes" laurel trades on the Cannes name [IS24]. Show Me Shorts lists names that closely resemble a well-known festival as a red flag [SMS].
- **Semi-finalist laurels.** At least one FilmFreeway festival gives semi-finalist laurels to the top 20% of each category, and those films are not screened [FF-SF].
- **Market listings presented as selections.** One example is the "Official Selection – Clermont-Ferrand Short Film Market" laurel [IS13]. Clermont's market catalogue lists every film entered in competition, whether selected or not [CLF-MKT].
- **Outdated names.** Distributor-made laurels in 2024 still say "Tribeca Film Festival". The festival dropped "Film" from its name in 2021 [TRI].
- "Submitted to" or "Official Submission" laurels: no festival rule addressing them was found. Advice sources treat any laurel that overstates the result as damaging to credibility [DSK], [SMS]. This is advice, not a rule.

### 2.3 Concrete guardrails for the generator

1. **Never ship festival logos, wordmarks or lookalike presets.** The logo slot accepts a user upload only, with the line: "Use the file the festival sent you." There should be no built-in "Sundance style" or "Cannes palm".
2. **Known-festival registry.** Keep a small, open JSON file of festivals with published rules (Cannes, Berlinale, Sundance, Giffoni to start), including aliases ("Sundance", "SFF", "Berlin"). When the festival field matches one, show a non-blocking notice: "This festival requires its own official artwork on your publicity," with the rule and source link. For Sundance, also flag the short-name rule and the size-parity rule. Users can still export, because the tool cannot verify and must not pretend to. Wording matters more than blocking.
3. **Status vocabulary.** Presets list only statuses festivals actually grant (Official Selection, Winner, Nominee, Finalist, Semi-Finalist, Honorable Mention, Special Mention, Audience Award, Jury Award, the premiere statuses). Leave "Submitted" and "Official Submission" out of the presets. If typed, show: "Laurels usually mark a result the festival gave you."
4. **Market and side-programme check.** If "Market", "Marché", "Short Film Corner", "Film Library" or "Video Library" sits next to "Official Selection", warn that markets are not selections.
5. **Exact wording.** Show a hint: "Use the festival's current official name and the exact wording it gave you," with Tribeca and Sundance as examples.
6. **No watermark, ever.** IMDb does not accept images with third-party branding or watermarks [IMDB]. A generator watermark baked into a poster would get the poster rejected.
7. **Privacy by default.** Everything stays client-side: no uploads, no public gallery, no telemetry on the text entered. Sundance asks films to keep the news confidential until its announcement [SUN-PDF].
8. **Row tools respect size-parity rules.** In a row containing an official laurel, offer "match the official laurel's size" and never auto-shrink it below the others [SUN-PDF].
9. **Plain disclaimer** in the footer and in exported file metadata: not affiliated with any festival, and the user is responsible for the claim.
10. **Usage note on export.** One line: laurels are for promoting the film itself; some festivals forbid them on merchandise [SUN-PDF].

### The gap a free, open, high-quality tool can own

- **Typography and anatomy.** No tool models the real field set (status, award, award type, section or strand, edition format, location, sponsor) or the year placements in §1.3. Every one stops at two or three text fields with one fixed layout.
- **Sets, not singles.** Filmmakers need rows and grids that share a size, style and colour. Every tool exports one laurel at a time. Tools for Film's own page tells users to compose rows in Canva [TFF-LC]. A filmmaker's blog describes cropping three laurels to identical pixel sizes before PicMonkey could line them up [GR].
- **Badge and wide forms.** No tool offers the branchless badge (the top-tier norm) or the wide flanking and U-wreath forms.
- **Production-grade export for free.** Outlined SVG, transparent PNG at any scale, vector PDF with CMYK, and video-ready alpha. Canva charges for transparency and SVG, Ste Bergin charges for PSD, and Laurel Designer Pro is PNG-only.
- **Honest guardrails.** No rival says anything about official artwork, trademarks or misleading statuses. Laurel Designer Pro sells "instant credibility" [LDP]. An open tool with a festival-rules registry and plain warnings would stand out.
- **Open source and private.** No rival is open source. Only Tools for Film says explicitly that it is client-side. An open repo of branch styles, layouts and the rules registry invites contributions.

## 4. Fonts

### 4.1 Delivery

| Route | Key? | What it gives | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Google Fonts Developer API** (`/webfonts/v1/webfonts`) [GF-API] | **Yes**, an API key on every request | Per family: family, variants, subsets, files (URLs per variant), category, lastModified, menu (a tiny file with just the family name's glyphs); `capability=VF` adds axes, `capability=WOFF2` returns WOFF2 | Good for building a full picker. The key must not ship in client code unless restricted, so run it at build time or through a proxy. |
| **Google Fonts CSS2 API** (`fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=…`) [GF-CSS2] | No | CSS with @font-face; axis ranges (`wght@200..900`, `ital,wght@…`); `display=swap`; `text=` returns a font with only the listed characters, "up to 90%" smaller | Fine for previews, but the browser then contacts Google (see privacy). |
| **Self-hosting** from `github.com/google/fonts` [GF-FAQ] or **Fontsource** | No | Fontsource's API returns family, weights, styles, variable flag, **licence** and TTF/WOFF/WOFF2 URLs per subset, with no key [FSRC] | Best fit: build a curated manifest at build time, self-host the WOFF2 for preview, and use the TTF that Fontsource serves alongside it for glyph outlining. |
| **Fontshare API** [FSH-API] | No | ITF fonts and some OFL fonts | ITF fonts **cannot** be offered in this tool (see 4.2). |

**Privacy.** Google states that requests to its Web API carry the visitor's IP address, user agent and referer, that it sets no cookies and does not profile users, and that self-hosting sends Google nothing [GF-FAQ]. In January 2022, the Munich Regional Court (LG München I, 3 O 17493/20) awarded €100 against a site that loaded Google Fonts from Google without consent. A wave of warning letters followed in Germany [LGM]. It is a single first-instance ruling, but it is a reason to **self-host everything**.

### 4.2 Licences, and whether outlining text in exports is OK

**OFL (nearly all Google Fonts, League of Moveable Type, most Velvetyne):**
- Artwork made with an OFL font is yours and is not covered by the OFL. OFL FAQ 1.1 and 1.1.1: "You remain the author and copyright holder of that newly derived graphic or object". 1.13: embedding or referencing a font in a document does not change the document's licence [OFL-FAQ].
- The licence text itself says the same: "The requirement for fonts to remain under this license does not apply to any document created using the Font Software" (condition 5) [OFL].
- Google's FAQ: "text in bitmap images or vector graphics made with fonts from Google Fonts can be modified, because the result isn't another font, but an image of text." Fonts may be used "within any logo" [GF-FAQ].
- **So converting text to outlines in SVG, PDF or PNG exports is fine.** The OFL FAQ has no item that names "convert to outlines" directly. The point is covered by 1.1, 1.13 and Google's FAQ line.
- Embedding the font in a PDF, in full or as a subset, is also allowed (FAQ 1.12) [OFL-FAQ].
- **Serving the fonts from the app** counts as distribution. Ship the copyright notice and licence with each font. The OFL fonts may not be sold on their own, which a free tool never does (conditions 1–2) [OFL].
- **Subsetting** a font yourself counts as modification. A font with a Reserved Font Name then cannot keep its name (FAQ 2.6–2.7) [OFL-FAQ]. Practical rule: serve the files unmodified, as published by Google or Fontsource, and do not make your own subsets of RFN fonts.

**ITF Free Font License v2.0 (17 Aug 2026), Fontshare's "Closed Source" fonts** [ITF]:
- Free for personal and commercial use. Self-hosting is allowed for your **own** sites and apps. Logos, wordmarks, images and vector files made with the fonts are allowed.
- **It explicitly forbids the generator's use case:** you may not make the fonts "available for use by third parties through any website, application, online service, SaaS platform, design tool, template editor or similar service. This includes making the Font Software available as a selectable font for third-party users to create, edit, customize or generate their own content, even where such users cannot download or extract the Font Software itself."
- It also forbids subsetting and format conversion. PDF embedding is allowed only where the font cannot be extracted.
- This covers Satoshi (which K180 uses), General Sans, Clash Display, Cabinet Grotesk, Switzer, Zodiak, Erode, Gambetta, Boska and others, all `itf_ffl` in Fontshare's API [FSH-API]. Fontshare's OFL-licensed families (e.g. Archivo, Manrope, Space Grotesk) are fine.
- **Verdict: do not offer any ITF FFL font in the public tool.**

**Other free sources:**
- **League of Moveable Type:** "free & open source … subject to the Open Font License" (League Gothic, League Spartan, Ostrich Sans, Raleway, Sorts Mill Goudy…) [LMT].
- **Velvetyne:** libre fonts with the OFL as the stated basis [VLV-FAQ], but **per-font licences vary**. Its GitHub repos are mostly OFL-1.1, with one GPL-2.0, one MIT and several unstated [VLV-GH]. Velvelyne uses the "CUTE" conditions, which restrict large companies [VLV-CUTE]. Include a Velvetyne font only after reading its bundled licence file.
- **Open Foundry:** a curated showcase ("Curated Open-Source Fonts") that mostly links to families such as Inter, Work Sans, Archivo, Oswald and EB Garamond; it is not a separate licence [OF]. Treat each font by its own licence.

**Bring your own font.** Letting users load a local font file, used only in their browser and never uploaded, sidesteps redistribution: the user's own licence governs. Exports must then **always be outlined** and must never embed the font file. An SVG with an embedded @font-face would hand the font on, which the ITF terms and most commercial EULAs forbid.

### 5.2 Do filmmakers need several laurels at once?

Yes. This is the main workflow, not an edge case.

- Posters carry rows and grids of laurels; one "Teacher of Patience" poster had about 20 [IS19], [IS24].
- Distributors redraw laurels from different festivals in one house style so a row matches (the Tribeca and London 2024 pair) [IS5], [IS16].
- Sundance's size-parity rule assumes several laurels sit together [SUN-PDF].
- Advice sources say to show fewer, stronger laurels, ordered by prestige and grouped by kind [DSK], [FF-MED].

### 5.3 Complaints and friction (no Reddit access; other forums and blogs)

1. **Colour and background.** Festival laurels arrive black on white. Inverting them produced a solid black background that blocked the poster, so a second tool was needed to make the background transparent [GR]. FilmQuest's answer is "add a Color Overlay in Photoshop" [FQ], which assumes Photoshop.
2. **Aligning a set.** Laurels came with uneven padding and had to be cropped to identical pixel sizes before they would line up [GR].
3. **Photoshop dependency.** The free packs (SeanFX, PremiumBeat) need Photoshop and manual recentring of text [SFX], [PB].
4. **Paywalls.** Transparent PNG and SVG cost money in Canva [CNV-TB]. Premium exports at Ste Bergin [SB].
5. **Legitimacy anxiety.** Filmmakers ask whether restyling or rewording a festival's laurel is allowed, and festivals give different answers [IT], [TAL], [FQ].
6. **Inflation fatigue.** Buyers and programmers are unimpressed by unfamiliar "Official Selection" laurels [SMS], and festivals that hand out too many awards "should raise some red flags" [GSH].

## Implications for the product

1. **Two families, one engine.** Support wreath laurels (open, wide, U or arch, custom material) **and** a branchless badge mode. The badge is how the top tier looks now and needs a large logo slot.
2. **The year is a movable token**, with presets for placements A–H. Default to A (own bottom line). One click moves it onto the status line (B) or after the name (C), or makes it a hero numeral (D). Support month + year and abbreviated years.
3. **Extend the data model** with: award type (Jury, Audience), strand or programme, edition in its own format (80 / 72nd / III), date range or month, sponsor line, slogan, venue or tour, second-language line, and an icon slot that can sit above or between words. Allow 5 text lines, not 4.
4. **Rows are first-class.** Build a set editor (row, grid, tiered) with shared style, an equal optical size and baseline, prestige ordering, and a "pin this laurel at least as large" control for official artwork.
5. **The official-artwork slot plus a rules registry** (§2.3) replaces any temptation to recreate famous laurels. The project ships no logos.
6. **Fonts: a curated, self-hosted OFL set** (§4.3) built from a Fontsource or Google manifest at build time, licences bundled, and no ITF FFL fonts. An optional local "bring your own font" that is always outlined on export.
7. **Exports, in order:**
   - SVG (outlined, layered, accessible title)
   - PNG with alpha at any scale
   - Vector PDF with CMYK and a spot-friendly single-colour mode
   - PNG sequence and WebM alpha for animated laurels
   - ProRes 4444 later, as an optional wasm module
   - No watermark, all client-side.
8. **Small-size truth.** Preview at FilmFreeway-poster scale (about 70 px wide) and at the Usage study's XS and inline sizes. Suggest sturdier branches and a type size floor when the user goes below them.
9. **Recolour-an-official-PNG utility.** Load a festival's black-on-white laurel, make it transparent and recolour it, but only for festivals that allow it (FilmQuest-style). It fixes the most common complaint without redrawing anyone's mark.
10. **Ship the rules registry, the branch styles and the layouts as open data** so festivals and designers can contribute. That is the open-source moat no rival has.
